# CISO AI Market Map

**Publisher:** Open Future Forum
**Research type:** Market map / vendor landscape, paired with first-party demand data
**Audience:** CISOs, security leaders, and security AI vendors
**Edition:** Edition 1
**Publication date:** July 2026
**Canonical source:** https://openfutureforum.com/research/ciso-ai-market-map

## Overview

The CISO AI Market Map is a companion to the CISO AI Leverage Report. It maps 63 security AI vendors across eight workflow areas (AI agent/model/LLM security, threat detection/response and AI SOC, identity and non-human identity, cloud exposure and validation, application/code/software supply chain security, data security and AI data leakage, GRC/compliance, and security automation), paired with first-party demand signals from 79 application-stage survey responses. As with the other market maps, vendors are grouped editorially and explicitly not scored or ranked.

## Methodology & Sample

The demand overlay draws on a small-base survey (40 any-mention responses on the "biggest AI security problem" and funding questions) alongside a broader applicant pool of 79 responses, of which 35 identified as security leads. The report is explicit that this reflects qualified interest rather than confirmed purchases, and that categories without a direct survey question (cloud exposure, AppSec, GRC) are noted as "thin and indirect" demand signals requiring vendor-specific proof.

## Key Findings

- Securing AI agents and their access was the clearest first-party demand signal, mentioned in 22 of 40 small-base responses — well ahead of any other security concern.
- Data leakage into AI models and shadow AI (unapproved AI tool use) tied as the next most-cited concerns, each appearing in 10 of 40 responses.
- Funding for AI security is split roughly evenly between having a dedicated budget line and buying case-by-case (14 of 40 any-mentions each), with 8 of 40 carving AI security spend from the existing security budget and 6 of 40 reporting no AI security spend at all.
- The vendor landscape shows identity and non-human identity (managing AI agent credentials, tokens, and service-account access) emerging as a critical adjacent category to agent security, even though the survey did not ask an identity-specific question directly.
- Several vendors span multiple categories (e.g., agent security platforms overlapping with identity management), reflecting how new agentic AI risk cuts across traditional security tooling boundaries.
- The report explicitly separates "strong" demand signals (agent/access security) from "thin and indirect" ones (cloud exposure, AppSec, GRC), giving vendors and buyers a calibrated read on where first-party proof exists versus where it must still be established.
