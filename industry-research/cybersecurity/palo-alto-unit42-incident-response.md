## Unit 42 Global Incident Response Report

**Publisher:** Palo Alto Networks (Unit 42)
**Topic:** Cybersecurity, AI-accelerated attacks, identity security, incident response
**Edition:** 2026 edition
**Source:** https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/resources/research/unit-42-incident-response-report

### Summary

Unit 42, Palo Alto Networks' threat intelligence and incident response arm, publishes an annual Global Incident Response Report based on its own investigation caseload. The 2026 edition identifies four major trends: AI as a force multiplier for attackers, identity as the most reliable path to attacker success, software supply chain risk expanding beyond vulnerable code to misuse of trusted connectivity, and nation-state actors adapting stealth and persistence tactics.

### Relevant Findings

- AI has become a measurable force multiplier for attackers: in 2025, exfiltration speeds for the fastest attacks quadrupled, and AI is compressing the overall attack lifecycle from initial access to impact while introducing new attack vectors.
- Identity weaknesses played a material role in almost 90% of Unit 42 investigations, with attackers increasingly using stolen credentials and tokens to "log in" rather than hack in, then exploiting fragmented identity estates to escalate privileges and move laterally.
- Software supply chain risk has expanded beyond vulnerable code to the misuse of trusted connectivity: attackers exploit SaaS integrations, vendor tools, and application dependencies to bypass perimeters at scale, shifting impact from isolated compromise to widespread operational disruption.
- Nation-state actors are adapting stealth and persistence tactics to modern enterprise environments, increasingly relying on persona-driven infiltration (fake employment, synthetic identities) and deeper, longer-term footholds.

### Relevant Themes

- AI-accelerated attack lifecycles and exfiltration speed
- Identity as the primary attacker success path
- Supply chain risk via trusted SaaS/vendor connectivity rather than just vulnerable code
- Nation-state persona-driven infiltration tactics
