# VC & CVC AI Investment Report

**Publisher:** Open Future Forum
**Research type:** Investor benchmark report (venture capital and corporate venture capital)
**Audience:** Venture capital investors, corporate and strategic investors, growth and seed funds, family offices, angels
**Edition:** Edition 1
**Publication date:** July 2026
**Canonical source:** https://openfutureforum.com/research/vc-cvc-ai-investment-report

## Overview

The VC & CVC AI Investment Report reads how venture capital and corporate venture capital investors are backing AI and where they see it already producing measurable value across their own portfolios. Its flagship metric, the AI Investor Conviction Index, is set alongside external venture-funding data from OECD, CB Insights, Silicon Valley Bank, Menlo Ventures, and MIT.

## What is the AI Investor Conviction Index?

The report's flagship metric: the share of investors who name at least one place AI already makes a measurable difference across their portfolio. In Edition 1 it reads 86% (base 237), an early, directional read.

## How much venture capital is going to AI?

AI captured 61% of global venture capital in 2025 ($258.7B of $427.1B) by OECD's count, and close to half of a record $469B venture year by CB Insights' count.

## Who owns the AI buying decision inside portfolio companies?

Investors most often name the CEO (51%), ahead of the CIO/CTO (24%) and finance (12%), pointing to AI purchases being decided as company-level bets rather than departmental ones (base 245).

## Are investors seeing measured returns yet?

Conviction is high, with 86% naming measurable value, but the external record is mixed: roughly 95% of enterprise AI pilots show no measured profit impact (MIT), even as 47% of AI deals reach production (Menlo Ventures).

## Methodology & Sample

First-party reads come from an application-stage instrument fielded across Open Future Forum investor, finance, and founder rooms in 2026, with more than 290 distinct applicants in the investor room. Every figure carries its base; multi-select figures are reported as any-mention and can sum past 100%. The flagship conviction question is entering the field in its sharpest form; the portfolio-impact read here is its labeled directional seed. Third-party figures are attributed and used for context only.
